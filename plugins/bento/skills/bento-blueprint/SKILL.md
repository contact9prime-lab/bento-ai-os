# Bento Blueprint

A blueprint gets to the bottom of a thing rather than presenting a chart about it. The
parts nest, so the person drills in on the page without waiting on you.

## Inside Bento

If you have a `show_scene` tool, call it with `kind: "blueprint"` and the fields below.
Bento fills in thin parts on its own afterwards, so show the outline once.

## Anywhere else

Outside Bento nothing fills the parts in later, so do the depth yourself:

1. Research the thing. Look up real dimensions, materials, figures, and name sources.
2. Write `blueprint.json` as a story with one blueprint scene (add a chart or timeline
   after it only if it helps):

   ```json
   {"question": "Give me a blueprint of the Burj Khalifa",
    "scenes": [{"kind": "blueprint", "title": "Burj Khalifa, taken apart",
                "subject": "Burj Khalifa", "form": "tower", "unit": "m",
                "source": "CTBUH; SOM", "parts": [...], "links": [...],
                "facts": [...], "unknowns": [...]}]}
   ```

3. Run `python3 ${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/render.py blueprint.json -o blueprint.html`.
4. Read every `note:` line. Dropped fields are named by their path (`parts[2].parts[0].colour`).

## What to give

Asked what is inside something, how a building, a machine, a body, an organisation or a system is built, or for a blueprint, make ONE blueprint scene the main scene: it takes the thing apart so the person can open any part on their screen. form is tower for something built in layers along a height (a building, a rocket, the earth; parts bottom first, with from and to as heights in unit, and width as how wide each part really is in the same unit, so the outline is drawn to scale; only when you cannot know it, 0 to 1 as a share of the widest), exploded for a thing made of parts round a core (an engine, a watch, a cell, a company) or flow for a process in order. Show the OUTLINE quickly: six to ten parts, each with its role, how it works (how), two or three facts, and the names of the parts inside it; the whole's own key numbers in facts; links between parts (from, to, a verb, and kind: what flows along it, one of load, power, fluid, heat, air, data, signal, control, material, money, people); what nobody outside can know in unknowns. Each part may say what it is made of (made_of), its limits or how it fails (limits), and how sure anyone is (certainty known, estimated or unclear). A child part may take another form (a tower's foundation drawn exploded). Never give a picture address in a blueprint. Give model too: what the thing looks like as five to thirty solids (the shapes of a model scene), each with part: the name of the part it draws, so the blueprint opens as a 3D drawing the person turns, pulls apart and taps into. A tower with heights is stacked in 3D for you.

Outside Bento, go further than the outline: give each of the six to ten top parts its own
`parts` (three to eight each), and the most interesting of those their own, so the drawing
holds two or three levels. Every part should have `role`, `how`, two or three `facts` and,
where it matters, `made_of`, `limits` and `certainty`.

## The closed set

- `form`: `tower`, `exploded`, `flow`. A tower's parts go bottom first, with `from` and `to` as heights in
  `unit`, and `width` as the real width in the same unit (0 to 1 only when you cannot know it).
- A part: `name`, `id`, `role`, `detail`, `how`, `made_of`, `limits`, `facts`, `from`, `to`, `width`, `certainty`, `status`, `source`, `evidence`, `form`, `parts`, `unknowns`, `steps`, `like`, `look_for`.
- `certainty`: `known`, `estimated`, `unclear`. It is drawn as the line (solid, dashed, dotted), never colour alone.
- `status` (for a system's parts): `ok`, `refused`, `asked`, `waiting`, `failed`, `stopped`, `running`.
- `facts`: `[{"label": "Height", "value": "828", "unit": "m"}]`, up to 6 a part and
  8 for the whole.
- `links`: `[{"from": "core", "to": "foundation", "verb": "carries load to", "kind": "load"}]`,
  `kind` one of `load`, `power`, `fluid`, `heat`, `air`, `data`, `signal`, `control`, `material`, `money`, `people`. `from` and `to` are part ids (or names).
- `steps` on a part: how it works, in order, up to 6.
- Limits: 4 levels, 12 parts at one level, 160 parts in all.
- Facts with numbers need `source` on the scene, or `"illustrative": true`.
