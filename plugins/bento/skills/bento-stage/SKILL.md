# Bento Stage

An answer shown, not only written. You pick three to six scenes, in story order, write
them as JSON, and the render script draws them with the same painter Bento's Stage uses.

## Inside Bento

If you have a `show_scene` tool, you are inside Bento: call it once per scene with the
same fields described below, before you write your reply. Skip the script.

## Anywhere else

1. Find the facts first. Real figures you looked up, with where they came from.
2. Write `story.json`:

   ```json
   {"question": "What the person asked",
    "answer": "Your written answer, plain text, paragraphs split by a blank line",
    "scenes": [ {"kind": "numbers", ...}, {"kind": "map", ...} ]}
   ```

3. Run `python3 ${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/render.py story.json -o story.html`.
4. Read every `note:` line it prints. A refused scene says why; fix it and run again.
5. Hand the person the page (open it, or give its path).

## How to tell the story

Anything worth seeing is shown: three to six scenes, in story order. Tell a story, not a slide: open on what matters most (the headline numbers, or a picture of the main thing), then the detail, and END on what it means for this person (what to do, what to watch, how it touches what they told you about themselves). Every caption is the takeaway as a sentence with a verb ('A stampede in Kolkata and a red alert in Chennai lead the day'), never a label ('Stories by location'). On a map, give every point a note saying what happened there, in a few words; set route: true only when the places are a journey. News: the top stories' key numbers, a map with a note on each place, a picture of the biggest story, and what it means for them. A how-to or a tutorial: the steps, a picture, and a YouTube video (search the web for the best tutorial on it and pass its youtube.com address as a video scene). A place: a map, and a picture of it. Numbers or a trend: a chart or key numbers. A process or a cause: steps or a timeline. Choose how big each answer is yourself: a fact gets none, a comparison a chart, and something that moves, grows or gets built (a launch, a factory, a city growing, a supply chain, a startup's first year) earns a film: a short 3D animation you write as three to six shots, each a subject and a camera move with the line said over it. Open or close a big story with one; never more than one film per answer. A picture is an https image address you found (a photo on Wikipedia or the maker's site). Use real figures you looked up and name the source; if a picture is only an example, set illustrative: true. A plain question (a fact, a yes or no, small talk) gets no scenes. Asked to build, imagine, design or show a place, a scene or an object (Kyoto in spring, a castle on a hill, a space station, a battle on a city street), show ONE model scene: it is drawn in ink on the sheet, rises into 3D and comes to life, and the person turns it round. You place solids: each {shape, at: [x, y, z] (x right, z towards the viewer, y the height of its base, 0 on the ground), size: [width, height, depth], color, label}. Shapes: box, cylinder, cone, pyramid, sphere, dome, roof, ring, arch to build anything, and ready-made tree, blossom, pine, palm, bamboo, bush, rock, mountain, cloud, water, house, building, pagoda, torii, bridge, lantern, wall, column, stairs, path, person, bird, boat, car, plane. count with spread: [width, depth] scatters one thing over an area (a grove of 30 blossom trees is one solid). turn is degrees round the vertical, tilt lays it over (90 is on its side along x, so an engine or a pipe is cylinders with tilt 90 on one axis; y is still its lowest point); move is one of spin, sway, bob, drift, orbit, flow, walk; tiers is the storeys of a pagoda or a building. color is one of pink, red, orange, gold, yellow, green, leaf, teal, blue, sky, navy, purple, brown, wood, stone, grey, white, black, sand, glass or #rrggbb. style is real (inked and raised on paper, then it comes to life in sunlight with cast shadows, the default), paper (ink on brown paper), blueprint or hologram (projected in the air); weather is one of petals, leaves, snow, rain, fireflies, sparks; ground one of grass, water, sand, snow, stone, space (space puts it among the stars with no ground: the sphere in the middle shines on the rest, and whatever has move orbit goes round it, the nearer the faster, its path drawn; a solar system is a sun at the middle and each planet a sphere at its distance, not to scale, Saturn with a ring at the same place). Compose it like a set designer: a ground to stand on, one main thing in the middle and named, supporting things round it, something that moves, 15 to 50 solids, sizes in metres. Label only what matters, five to eight things, and give each a note: one sentence the tour says when the eye stops there (what it is, why it matters). People, boats and cars move on their own. For a real object that people make or that grows (a chair, a table, a lamp, a plant, a rock, a barrel, a statue), also give look_for: two or three plain words for it, the noun last ("wooden armchair"). A real model is looked up and stands where your solid is, at your size; your solid stays as its plan, so still choose the nearest shape. Not for buildings, vehicles or people: none are in the library yet. A blueprint (a thing taken apart) is its own skill, bento-blueprint.

## The rules the script holds you to

- A scene is a closed set. Fields it does not know are dropped and named in a `note:`.
- **Numbers say where they came from.** A chart, a map with values, key numbers and a
  table need `source` (a site, a dataset or a URL), or `"illustrative": true` when they
  are an example. The page prints "Illustrative, not data" on those. Never invent figures.
- At most 8 scenes. Every scene can carry `title`, `caption` (the
  takeaway as a sentence with a verb), `say` (a line to read aloud) and `source`.
- Pictures are `https` addresses you found (Wikipedia, the maker's site). Videos are a
  youtube.com or youtu.be address. Nothing else is fetched.

## Scene kinds

- `chart`: `chart_type`, `series`, `x_label`, `y_label`, `unit`, `marks`
- `map`: `points`, `regions`, `view`, `unit`
- `numbers`: `items`
- `timeline`: `events`
- `steps`: `items`, `ordered`
- `table`: `columns`, `rows`
- `image`: `url`, `asset`, `alt`, `credit`
- `video`: `url`, `start`
- `film`: `shots`, `look`
- `blueprint`: `subject`, `form`, `unit`, `parts`, `links`, `unknowns`, `facts`, `look_for`, `model`, `style`
- `model`: `solids`, `style`, `weather`, `ground`

Shapes, field by field:

- **chart**: `chart_type` one of `line`, `area`, `bar`, `scatter`, `pie`; `series`: `[{"name": "Price", "points": [["2024", 12.5], ["2025", 14]]}]`
  (up to 8 series); `x_label`, `y_label`, `unit`; `marks`: `[{"y": 15, "text": "target"}]`.
- **map**: `points`: `[{"lat": 25.3, "lon": 51.5, "label": "Doha", "value": 46, "note": "what happened here"}]`;
  `regions`: `[{"country": "India", "value": 12, "label": "..."}]`; `view` one of `world`, `africa`, `asia`, `europe`, `north america`, `south america`, `oceania`, `middle east`, `india`;
  `unit`; `route: true` only when the places are a journey.
- **numbers**: `items`: `[{"label": "Revenue", "value": "2.6", "unit": "$bn", "delta": "+12%", "note": "..."}]`
  (up to 12). A rise or fall is read from the sign of `delta`.
- **timeline**: `events`: `[{"when": "1969", "label": "Apollo 11", "note": "..."}]` (up to 40).
- **steps**: `items`: `[{"title": "...", "text": "...", "note": "..."}]`; `ordered: false` for a list.
- **table**: `columns`: `["City", "Temp"]`; `rows`: `[["Doha", "46"]]` (up to 8 columns, 60 rows).
- **image**: `url`, `alt`, `credit`.
- **video**: `url` (YouTube), `start` in seconds.
- **film**: `look` one of `day`, `dusk`, `night`; `shots`: up to 6 of
  `{"subject": one of `city`, `tower`, `bars`, `rocket`, `factory`, `solar`, `road`, `conveyor`, `crowd`, `globe`, "camera": one of `orbit`, `push`, `rise`, `fly`, `top`, "seconds": 4, "text": "the line said over it"}`.
  Outside Bento a film is drawn as its storyboard.
- **model**: a place, a scene or an object built in 3D. `solids`: up to 80 of
  `{"shape": ..., "at": [x, y, z], "size": [width, height, depth], "color": ..., "label": "Pagoda", "note": "one sentence the tour says here"}`
  (x right, z towards the viewer, y the height of its base). Shapes to build anything: `box`, `cylinder`, `cone`, `pyramid`, `sphere`, `dome`, `roof`, `ring`, `arch`;
  ready-made: `tree`, `blossom`, `pine`, `palm`, `bamboo`, `bush`, `rock`, `mountain`, `cloud`, `water`, `house`, `building`, `pagoda`, `torii`, `bridge`, `lantern`, `wall`, `column`, `stairs`, `path`, `person`, `bird`, `boat`, `car`, `plane`. `count` with `spread: [width, depth]` scatters one thing over an area;
  `turn` and `tilt` in degrees; `move` one of `spin`, `sway`, `bob`, `drift`, `orbit`, `flow`, `walk`; `tiers` for storeys. `color` one of
  `pink`, `red`, `orange`, `gold`, `yellow`, `green`, `leaf`, `teal`, `blue`, `sky`, `navy`, `purple`, `brown`, `wood`, `stone`, `grey`, `white`, `black`, `sand`, `glass` or `#rrggbb`. `style` one of `real`, `paper`, `blueprint`, `hologram`; `weather` one of `petals`, `leaves`, `snow`, `rain`, `fireflies`, `sparks`;
  `ground` one of `grass`, `water`, `sand`, `snow`, `stone`, `space`. Label five to eight things and give each a note.
- **blueprint**: a thing taken apart. Use the `bento-blueprint` skill for it.

## What the page does in 3D

The page carries its own 3D engine, so it needs no network. A model, a bar, line or area chart
and a map are built in front of the reader: the plan is inked on paper, it rises, then it comes
to life with sunlight and cast shadows (`style: real`, the default; `paper`, `blueprint` and
`hologram` are the other looks, one tap away). Drag turns it, **Tour** goes round the named
things and shows each note, **Clip** saves a video of the build with sound, and on a chart or a
map **Flat** brings back the flat drawing for reading values closely. So: give every map place
and every model label a `note`, because the tour reads them, and keep a chart's category names
short, because each bar carries its name and value.
